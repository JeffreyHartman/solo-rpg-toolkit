# Ironsworn Oracles

Oracles inject randomness into the narrative. When the agent needs to answer a question that isn't preordained by the fiction, it rolls on an oracle and **interprets the result** rather than inventing.

*(Ironsworn oracles are by Shawn Tomkin under CC BY 4.0. The Action and Theme oracles below are the full published lists.)*

## How to use oracles

- **Action + Theme** is the Swiss Army knife. Roll both (1d100 each), then interpret the pair in context. "Persevere + Energy" could be "a figure straining to keep a magical fire from going out." Trust the first creative read — don't re-roll until you get something you like.
- **Yes/No**: If Mythic GME is loaded, use Fate Check. Otherwise use the Ask the Oracle thresholds in `moves.md`.
- **Plot Turn**: When the story feels stuck or needs a shake-up, roll a Plot Turn oracle instead of inventing one.
- **Names/places**: Roll when introducing something new.

When you roll, tell the player what you rolled and what you rolled on. Transparency is the whole point. "Rolling Action/Theme to see what's in the next room… I got Betray + Bond. Hmm — one of your old companions is here, and something in their behavior feels off."

## Plot Turn (d100) — roll when the story feels stagnant

Use together with Action/Theme for nuance. Common patterns:
1-10: An ally betrays or disappoints.  
11-20: An enemy reveals unexpected depth, history, or humanity.  
21-30: A secret is revealed (roll Descriptor/Focus for the shape of the secret).  
31-40: A stranger arrives bearing news or a request.  
41-50: A past promise or bond reasserts itself.  
51-60: Resources run short unexpectedly.  
61-70: A place the character thought safe becomes dangerous.  
71-80: An old enemy resurfaces.  
81-90: A new faction makes its presence known.  
91-100: The environment itself turns — weather, terrain, or magic.  

(This is a compact version. Use it as a starter; interpret loosely.)

## How the agent should narrate oracle results

1. Announce the roll and result: *"Action/Theme: 67 + 45 → Advance / Seize."*
2. Briefly interpret: *"Something is pushing forward to take hold of a resource or position."*
3. Commit: narrate that thing happening in fiction, concretely.
4. Hand back to player: "What do you do?"

Avoid the temptation to re-roll until you get something "cool." The dissonance of an unexpected result is where solo play gets interesting.
