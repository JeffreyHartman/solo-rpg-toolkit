# The player controls the character; the Referee controls the world

The player changes their own character through direct actions in the harness: edit the sheet and the inventory, move gear, move the token to a known area, and roll their own checks. No Referee turn runs for a direct action. The Referee controls the world: monsters, NPCs, what an area holds, rulings, and the pace. It takes a Referee turn only when the world must answer. We chose this split because the first playtest showed a harness where the player could change nothing without a chat message, while the target is a table like a Foundry game with a human GM. The alternative was to keep every change behind the Referee, which costs a plan turn per click and puts the player in the role of a chat user.

## Consequences

- The engine logs every direct action, and the Referee reads that log at the start of its next turn.
- The UI needs an engine command for each direct action. The Referee tools stay as ADR 0001 describes them.
- A direct action is limited to the player's own party. A change to the world, for example new treasure in an area, still goes through a procedure or the Referee.
